How it works

Three steps, then it is just there

No connecting accounts, no waiting for a sync. The whole loop runs on files you already have.

  1. 01

    Export a statement

    Download the statement your bank already offers — .txt, .csv or .pdf. No credentials are ever asked for.

  2. 02

    Drop it in

    Pick the account, drop the file. Duplicates are skipped, so overlapping ranges are safe. Or point it at a folder and forget about it.

  3. 03

    See where the money went

    Transactions are categorized by merchant on the way in. Trends, budgets, bills and monthly reports follow from there.

Where your data goes

Nowhere

Claims worth checking rather than taking on faith — every one of them is visible in the source.

No account to create

You choose a username on first run and it is stored on your machine. There is no sign-up and no server holding it.

No bank connection

It never asks for net banking credentials and never talks to your bank. It reads files you already have.

Nothing is uploaded

The database and your statements sit in a folder beside the program. Unplug the network and it behaves identically.

Readable source

The code is public. If you would rather not trust a downloaded binary, build it yourself.

About

Why local-first

01

Your data never leaves the machine

Financial records are the obvious case, but it holds generally: software that keeps your files on your own disk cannot lose them in someone else's breach.

02

One install, no dependencies

Everything a product needs is inside the installer — runtime, database, web server. Nothing to configure before it works.

03

It keeps working

No subscription to lapse and no server to shut down. An installed copy runs for as long as the machine does.
